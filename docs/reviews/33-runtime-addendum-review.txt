F1 disposition: CLOSED
Operation identity equals command correlation within stable principal/aggregate
scope; diagnostic request identity stays separate. Fingerprints include command,
target, content digests and revision/evidence preconditions. Serialized recognition
prevents concurrent candidates. Authorized retained committed matches return their
historical outcome without executing or imposing today's revision fence; mismatched
content conflicts. Definitive noncommit and uncertain evidence remain distinct.
Co-committed recognition survives restart. Published retention boundaries do not
turn absent, expired or damaged evidence into failure, including inside the stated
horizon. Retry mode cannot become first submission. Explicit new intent exposes
possible duplication; deliberate first-submission misuse is outside this bounded
contract. Storage, wire and client behavior now agree without global exactly-once
or power-loss claims.

F2 disposition: CLOSED UNDER STATED PRECONDITIONS
Responsibility now rests with the issuer/control plane, launcher, Go adapter and
browser. Captured private IPC associates the intended generation/key; launcher
verification of chain, exact name and expected key precedes grant registration.
The browser receives no URL secret and discloses the entered grant only after
trust/name verification at the launcher-selected origin. Atomic single-use exchange
binds principal, instance, origin, scope and pending browser nonce to a new session.
Protected reads, commands and fetch-stream events require cookie plus session-bound
header and browser-context checks; public navigation grants no protected access.
Current authority/lease checks, stream invalidation and acknowledged-or-expired
revocation precede replacement; lifecycle quiescence still applies. Lost exchange
responses require new issuance, never command replay. These close the prior missing
handshake, rather than merely naming another unspecified owner.
