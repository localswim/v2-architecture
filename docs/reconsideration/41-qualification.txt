LOCALSWIM REASSESSMENT - QUALITY, EVIDENCE AND ADOPTION (#0041)
2026-10-25 | Recommendation only | Author: sol_medium_0041_qualification

Outcome
Change the qualification sequence: establish essential outcomes and acceptable
loss first, run a few distinguishing experiments, then qualify the chosen slice.
Retain independent review and controlled adoption. Neither functioning v1 nor
prior v2 acceptance demonstrates that the proposed architecture is necessary,
affordable or qualified. Language, persistence, topology and trust choices remain
open under the reassessment charter.

Evidence and decision inventory
Public v1 README behavior describes concurrent browser/CLI work, multiple boards,
recoverable errors, focused reads, archive, attachments, opt-in publishing and
sanitized monitoring. This supports real collaboration needs; it supplies no
measured workload distribution, support cost or performance baseline. Working v1
is additional owner evidence of usefulness, not proof that every behavior matters.

Public v2 candidates 01/03/25/26/28 propose a broad capability baseline,
Windows11/Linux x64, selected desktop browsers, directly attached storage,
provisional capacity/latency budgets, layered testing, separated coexistence and
controlled migration. Their assumptions and numerical targets remain unverified.
The proposed Go/TS7 split, issuer provisioning, generator boundary and recovery
machinery add qualification obligations; they are choices to justify, not fixed
entry requirements for comparing alternatives.

Missing requirements with greatest decision impact:
- Which daily jobs must the first deliverable complete, including existing
  automation scripts, multi-board attention and attachment work?
- Who uses it, on which actual machines/browsers/storage, with how many concurrent
  actors and what representative board/attachment sizes?
- What acknowledged loss, recovery time, draft loss and operator interruption are
  acceptable? Is metadata history sufficient or must prior prose be recoverable?
- What delivery date, maintenance capacity and installation/support effort can
  Terry fund? Current candidates specify resource ceilings without a development
  or operational cost budget.
- Which privacy/access threats must be resisted beyond cooperating processes
  under a trusted desktop account? This determines whether trust complexity buys
  a required outcome.

Approaches
Approach | Benefit | Loss / transition cost
Full v2 baseline before adoption | Broad replacement at one boundary | Long evidence path; migration waits for every subsystem
Staged outcome slices (prefer) | Earlier learning and bounded support | Temporary feature gaps, parallel operation and later expansion
Retain v1 while testing alternatives | Lowest immediate disruption | Continued incumbent maintenance; replacement benefits delayed

Retain / change / unresolved
Retain: prevent silent lost updates, preserve refused/uncertain user intent,
protect private content, make save/publish/recovery states honest, keep healthy
boards usable, and preserve human approval boundaries. These are useful acceptance
outcomes independent of language, protocol and storage. Retain synthetic fault
fixtures, real interaction evidence where needed and fresh independent review.

Change: replace blanket behavioral parity with a short outcome ledger. For each
important journey record required continuity, proposed improvement, intentional
loss, rationale and smallest acceptance demonstration. Exact commands, routes,
layouts and numerical formulas merit preservation only where users or automation
depend on them. A changed triage algorithm needs understandable examples and
owner agreement, not a legacy implementation oracle.

One concrete regression needs disposition: v1 documents session-storage drafts
surviving reload; v2 28 offers memory-only navigation retention. Memory-only
drafts may reduce retained private data and complexity, but lose reload recovery.
Treat that as an explicit tradeoff; test a restart during an unsent comment and
obtain a retention/privacy decision before calling the new browser acceptable.

Unresolved: numerical budgets and platform scope. Keep 03's numbers as hypotheses.
Select an initial target from actual usage evidence; do not require Linux and
three browsers merely because they appear in a candidate. Narrowing loses reach
and requires an explicit unsupported-target statement.

Smallest distinguishing evidence, in order
1. Owner-ranked journey examples and synthetic small/typical/stress fixtures:
   establish required outcomes, loss limits and an effort ceiling. No private
   board inspection is necessary for this proposal.
2. A later authorized disposable vertical slice: open, edit, conflict, restart
   and reconcile one board through CLI/browser. Compare the preferred design
   against the simplest credible alternative for the disputed boundary; measure
   install steps, latency, memory, recovery and implementation effort.
3. Targeted spikes only where they could change a decision: commit-interruption
   recovery, concurrent ownership and actual browser bootstrap. Test on the
   initial real target; record failures and operator burden. Do not build a
   universal harness or full migration tool to choose an architecture.
4. Expand platform, accessibility, scale and worker qualification as each slice
   enters release scope. Safety claims require their relevant evidence before use.

Adoption and proportional review
Prefer one explicit new-board or synthetic pilot before conversion. Defer costly
compatibility machinery until its required destinations and scripts are known.
For later authorized migration, retain separate writable scopes, source backup,
semantic mapping/loss report, isolated rehearsal and one named authority at cutover.
Avoid dual writing. Rollback before new writes can restore the old authority;
after new writes it requires reconciliation or an explicit accepted loss, not
merely swapping executables.

Use one concise decision/evidence record per disputed boundary and one fresh
review per bounded slice. Review safety-critical recovery/concurrency deeply;
batch low-risk documentary checks. This reduces handoff overhead while retaining
independence. Terry approves scope and intentional losses; no recommendation here
authorizes execution, installation, migration or adoption.
