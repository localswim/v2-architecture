PRODUCT REASSESSMENT - #0034
2026-10-25 | sol_medium_0034_product | Recommendation for fresh review

Decision
Prefer a focused collaboration product with selective behavioral continuity.
Neither feature parity nor a cards-only rewrite has earned selection. Functioning
v1 establishes feasibility and documented behaviors; it does not establish how
often every feature helps or whether its accumulated rules are desirable.
Everything below is proposed, including departures from prior v2 recommendations.

Evidence boundary
Observed requirement: Terry's instructions demonstrate scoped work, dependency
ordering, attributable automation, human-controlled acceptance and several boards.
README evidence [R] documents behavior, not measured usage, demand or correctness.
Prior v2 notes [V] are candidate designs with disclosed source-derived provenance.
No legacy source/tests/internal design or real boards were consulted. This work
does not establish isolated clean-room certification or approve asset reuse.
There are no new external technical/version claims or executed experiments here.

Major choices: need, alternatives and disposition
Choice | Evidence versus assumption | Recommendation and cost
Core cards, comments, search, stable references, browser/CLI | Documented [R]; human/automation collaboration explicit | RETAIN outcomes: create, find, edit, discuss and hand off; redesign syntax/layout. Removing CLI adds manual automation work; preserving every command adds qualification burden.
Workflow and actors | Configurable lanes/rights documented; exactly two actors and universal human/bot classes are legacy constraints | CHANGE to configurable identities, named lanes/priorities and grants, with a simple supplied template. Keep acceptance authority explicit; defer a general role/rule language. More configuration requires understandable defaults.
Relationships, hierarchy, triage | Documented [R]; particular formula and automatic priority rewriting lack fresh demand evidence | RETAIN navigable dependencies; UNRESOLVED hierarchy breadth and ranking. Compare manual priority plus dependency display, suggested ordering, and persisted automatic promotion. Simpler options lose automatic ordering; promotion changes user intent and requires explanation.
Archive and history | Reversible hiding and attribution documented; complete prose replay unproven | RETAIN archive/restore and resolvable references. UNRESOLVED full content history, retention and deletion; see below.
Attachments, covers, publishing, monitor, workspace | Documented [R]; equal launch importance assumed in v2 | RETAIN as candidates with staged scope decisions, not mandatory simultaneous parity. Omitting attachments loses evidence sharing; workspace omission fragments attention; monitor omission loses automation wakeups; publishing omission changes backup habits. Covers/pin refinements have a weaker demonstrated case than these outcomes.

Configurability without building a workflow platform
Compare fixed Terry lanes (lowest setup, poor reuse), declarative configuration
(recommended middle), and arbitrary scripted policy (broad flexibility, added authoring
and support burden). Terry's P3 default, In Progress gate, RFR/Needs Terry/Blocked
meanings and Completed authority remain binding operating instructions today.
Future product defaults should not require those labels or encode Terry as a
special principal. A template can teach the distinctions; software cannot prove
that off-board work obeyed the start gate. Configurable permissions must still
prevent a prohibited acceptance, rather than merely hide its button.
Multiple humans/bots should not be excluded just because v1 documents two; nor
should advanced group administration become a first-release requirement.

Archive, evidence and recovery
Compare current state plus attributable metadata, revisioned content history, and
complete event replay. Prefer the first as the minimum proposal, with explicit
limits: it cannot recover previous prose. Before accepting that loss, ask whether
correcting an accidental description edit is a required daily recovery task.
Revisioned content could meet that need without making event replay a product goal.

Archive is visibility, not completion or approval. Preserving an archived unfinished
dependency can explain outstanding work but surprise users who hide it; require an
explanation of hidden prerequisites. Whether archive should affect urgency remains
open alongside triage. Permanent retention prevents accidental erasure but adds
privacy/capacity burden. A deliberate later purge/export policy deserves evaluation;
this reassessment authorizes no deletion. Existing restore-roadmap code stays
excluded as input; the user need for restore deserves consideration independently.
Git publication, backup and user-visible history are separate outcomes; selecting
one should not silently promise the other two.

Compatibility and adoption
Prefer selective semantic mapping over exact legacy compatibility or a wholly fresh
start. Preserve accepted references, meaning and provenance when adoption includes
existing work; do not promise schema, URL, CLI, JSON, sorting, file-layout or visual
identity. A new syntax needs a concrete usability/maintenance benefit, not novelty.
Conversely, deliberate small compatibility shims may cost less than rewriting every
launcher and bookmarked link. Scope those from a synthetic representative workflow,
not an inventory of old internals.

A reduced launch may serve new boards while existing users retain v1; that does not
prove migration readiness. Any later conversion must report losses, preserve source
evidence and offer a reviewed fallback. Continuing v1 remains a valid outcome while
benefits do not repay adoption effort. No automatic migration or dual authority is
proposed.

Smallest distinguishing evidence
Use one newly written synthetic walkthrough: create/relate/prioritize work, bot
handoff, human acceptance, archive/reopen, accidental text edit, attachment evidence,
and cross-board attention. Compare the three triage approaches and two history
depths using outcome predictability, steps and unexplained changes. Record which
optional features prevent a real task, rather than whether they existed before.
Obtain Terry's launch-critical subset and compatibility needs from that walkthrough;
no new standing gate process or implementation is authorized.
