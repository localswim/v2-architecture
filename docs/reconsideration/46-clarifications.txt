43F1 - reload preserves submitted uncertainty separately from drafts
Recommend two recovery classes. An unsent draft remains editable: recover it across
navigation/reload where permitted, label its base revision, and disclose persistence
failure. Memory-only loses reload recovery; longer retention increases private
content exposure and cleanup cost. Bounded per-window recovery is preferred;
retention duration and deletion policy remain decisions, not claimed guarantees.

Before any submission, preserve a private reload-recoverable record of the immutable
submitted intent: operation identity, principal/board, command, exact fingerprint
inputs/preconditions, and sufficient original content to explain the action. Keep
this separate from the subsequently editable draft and from credentials. If that
record cannot be preserved, refuse submission with input intact; disclose this
availability/privacy cost. No particular browser storage technology is mandated.

On reload, recovered unresolved submissions remain pending/unknown. Reauthenticate,
then perform authorized lookup using the original identity; never submit, replay,
or generate a replacement identity during recovery. Committed evidence can close
the record without applying the action again; definitive noncommit allows an
explicit new submission. Missing, expired or inaccessible evidence stays unknown.
Keep unresolved records until resolution or deliberate, warned discard; no automatic
expiry that silently turns submitted prose into a fresh draft. Device/storage loss
can destroy the handle and may be undetectable. When known, disclose recovery loss,
require manual investigation and warn that deliberate new intent could duplicate
the effect. Neither retained
prose nor visual similarity proves noncommit. Numeric limits remain unresolved.

43F2 - count only the actual monitor delta
40's native committed activity plus catch-up is a change from documented v1
filesystem-triggered monitoring, not a new simplification over candidate21.
21 already has committed signals, periodic reconciliation, optional filesystem hints
and a host-owned monitor scope. Its captured-child continuity makes process placement
a distinct question: host ownership alone removes no coordination obligations.

Recommend evaluating an in-process scheduler against that managed-child candidate.
It could remove separate child launch/discovery, child identity and child handover
mechanisms. It retains per-board exclusive delivery ownership, durable pending and
checkpoints, captured targets, conservative attribution, unknown delivery handling,
quiescent shutdown and adapter reconciliation. Host crash then stops monitoring;
a faulty/stalled adapter can harm host availability. Managed/separate workers retain
lifecycle and upgrade cost but can provide a useful isolation boundary; independent
monitor uptime additionally needs an independent usable activity reader. Optional
filesystem hints are already optional in21: omitting them saves watcher machinery,
losing prompt notice of unadmitted disk edits. Preserve storage-health detection
and honest uncertainty. No process savings or uptime advantage was measured here.

43F3 - acceptance concurrency and later invalidation are different
06 already rejects concurrent content/policy change while committing acceptance
and already limits later invalidation to substantive edits, leaving classification
open. Retract39's implication of an established blanket subsequent-invalidation rule.
Retain the atomic acceptance fence: accepted evidence identifies reviewed content
and policy; a race requires renewed review, never blind replay. Separately decide
which later changes invalidate current acceptance while preserving its history.
Compare whole-card invalidation (simple, extra review) with a reviewed-content
boundary (fewer nuisance reviews, classification/maintenance cost). Criteria
replacement, attachment replacement and a link changing scope can alter meaning;
a typo might not. Ambiguity should require review pending an agreed boundary.

43F4 - workspace attention and preferences
Recommend retaining a compact multi-board attention view, independently refreshed
and authorized, with unknown/stale distinct from zero; keep healthy boards usable
during a member outage. Compare a minimal chooser: less aggregation work, but
finding the next human-action board requires opening boards and can hide unavailable
work. This loss needs owner choice; no exact columns or ordering are prescribed.

Retain candidate17's separation provisionally: principal/workspace shared pins and
saved defaults provide continuity across windows but add preference persistence,
conflicts and stale-reference handling. Device/window-only preferences reduce shared
state but lose shared curation. Current route, temporary filters, focus/scroll and
private draft/submission context stay per-window, so one window cannot redirect
another's work. Pins grant no rights. Shared pin order is optional; authorizing
membership and board reads remains separate from either preference choice.

43F5 - inspection when the service is stopped
Prefer explicit shared-core stopped-board inspect/verify provisionally: useful
outage diagnosis without running the host. This retains tooling, format compatibility,
physical exclusion and recovery/authority qualification costs from15; listener
absence proves no ownership. Verification must distinguish evidence-preserving
checks from any required recovery writes; neither silently repairs nor becomes
an online mutation fallback.

Host-only reads simplify ownership and tooling but lose inspection during host
failure; alternative is restart/repair or inspect a labeled consistent export.
Export-only inspection avoids opening live authority but may be stale and cannot
verify its current integrity or unresolved operations. Keep these losses visible;
no stopped-board mutation capability is justified by this read/verify recommendation.
