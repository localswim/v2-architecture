novelty-driven breaks. CLI, dependencies, archive, stable references and attribution
have recognizable collaboration outcomes. Configurable identities/grants are a
proposal rather than proof of multi-user demand; arbitrary policy scripting and

choice, with v1 continuation and adoption costs visible.

36 makes a defensible complexity argument for transactional authority without
inventing throughput results. It preserves conflict checks, attributable admission,
unknown outcomes and external-byte obligations; a database is not presented as
solving identity, arbitrary retention or complete recovery. Keeping JSON as the
winner if direct canonical inspection/editing proves essential is a serious
alternative. Event-derived authority has no demonstrated necessity here.

37 correctly exposes the old launcher assertion's assurance limit. Cooperative
agent restrictions and adversarial exclusion are different promises. Its stronger
option explicitly includes protection of authoritative state/policy, not just a
human credential. HTTPS, HTTP and desktop IPC are compared without claiming any
transport proves a human reviewed the work. This is a material improvement over
retaining issuer/origin machinery by inheritance.
