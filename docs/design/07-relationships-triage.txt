Conceptual rule matrix
Concern | Retained outcome / recommended precise contract
Blocking | A blocks B means A is prerequisite, B downstream. blocked_by is the inverse presentation of the same fact. Only blocking links affect urgency/counts; relationship creation/type replacement is atomic.
Nonblocking | Typed directional associations remain visible and navigable; no urgency, count, execution eligibility or parenthood follows from them. Exact type vocabulary is deferred to the contract design.
Hierarchy | Separate organizational parent/child relation; recommend one parent per card, no self-parent or ancestor cycle. Reparent atomically. It neither creates blockers nor aggregates child completion/priority. Depth includes archived/completed members; no historical links disappear.
Blocking cycles | Permit and expose cycles, including mutual blocking; reject self-links as a proposed validation rule. Cycles do not make traversal undefined or count the origin. A cycle warns about execution deadlock; it never grants permission to start work.
Completion | Use a stable semantic completed state, independent of label. Completed nodes neither count, receive automatic promotions nor transmit urgency/reachability. Completing a card preserves links and never demotes descendants. Authorized reopening resumes the unfinished rules atomically.
Archive | Visibility only: preserve lane, identity, links, priority and history. Archived unfinished nodes participate fully. Hidden/search/lane filters and result limits never change global derived values. Explicit inspection can explain hidden participants.

Urgency and ordering recommendation
Preserve documented direction: an unfinished blocked card must be at least as urgent
as every unfinished upstream blocker reachable without crossing completion. This is
not reverse propagation from an urgent dependent to its prerequisites. Use the board
policy's total priority ranking; lower rank means greater urgency. Priority differences
never forbid a link: promote affected unfinished cards instead. Open cycle members
share the strongest applicable urgency. Removing links, completing cards or lowering
a blocker never silently demotes existing priorities. An explicit priority request is
bounded by remaining upstream floors; report requested and effective priority.

Recommend the documented score: ((priority rank + 1) * 100) minus the number of
distinct unfinished downstream cards reachable through blocking links. Count each
card once globally, exclude the origin, and stop at completion. Permit negative
scores. Order ascending score, then priority rank, then unique ticket number.
This can put a sufficiently influential lower-priority card first; the constant 100
is a product choice, not a guarantee of strict priority grouping.

Ownership and atomicity
Value | Authority / persistence boundary
Links, parent, priority, lane/completion, archive, policy rank | Persist accepted facts; Go domain authority validates a revision-checked board transaction and records attributable audit evidence. No separate persisted base/effective priority pair is proposed.
Promotions and explicit priority effects | Persist resulting priorities plus one attributable history effect per actually changed card with the triggering edit, including unselected/hidden cards, in one atomic board revision. Refusal/conflict changes neither facts nor audit; no blind retry.
Counts, floors, score, ordering, hierarchy depth | Derived from one committed board revision and identified policy. Service supplies authoritative values/explanations to web and CLI. Stopped-board reads use the same Go domain semantics without writes; TypeScript does not invent a second formula.

The transaction covers relationship/type edits, priority edits, authorized reopen and
policy changes whenever they affect urgency. Board-wide validation and audited
promotions succeed together or fail together; physical persistence is #0005's remit.
Recommend explicit multi-card reprioritization evaluated together: selected cycle
members can lower together, while unselected upstream blockers still impose floors.
Exact limits, commands, schema and audit representation belong to later contracts.

Synthetic acceptance examples (P0 most urgent; arrows mean blocks)
1. A -> B -> A: A counts B once; B counts A once. If A=P1 and B=P3,
   B promotes to P1. A self-link is refused under the proposed rule.
2. A -> B, A -> C, B -> D, C -> D: A counts three, not four.
3. A -> Completed B -> C: A counts zero; urgency does not cross B.
4. A -> archived unfinished B -> C: A counts two even when B is hidden.
5. A=P1 -> B=P3: B persists P1; lowering A to P4 leaves B=P1 until an
   explicit permitted change. B=P0 does not promote A.
6. A parent-of B, A relates-to C: neither relation changes triage or urgency.

Choices, evidence and limits
Recommend continuity above over strict-priority-first ordering; the latter is simpler
to explain but changes documented influence ranking. Rejecting blocking cycles would
simplify scheduling but reject documented graphs. Single-parent acyclic hierarchy and
self-link rejection are new proposals requiring confirmation; multi-parent hierarchy
is the bounded alternative and complicates organizational depth/ownership.
