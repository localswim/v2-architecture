LOCALSWIM REBOOT — DOMAIN AND WORKFLOW POLICY (#0006)
2026-10-25 | Author: sol_medium_0006_domain_policy
Architecture recommendation for independent #0009 review; no implementation authority.

Outcome and authority
Recommend a board-scoped domain with stable references, configurable workflow and
centrally enforced actor authority. Go owns domain decisions for service, CLI and
background tools; the TypeScript 7.x browser presents those decisions. Public
repositories remain MIT FOSS; actual board/runtime data remains private.
Terry's explicit #0004 acceptance releases this design wave despite its RFR lane.

Conceptual responsibilities
Entity / boundary       | Responsibility
Board                   | Stable identity; cards, workflow and configuration scope.
Card                    | Stable identity/ticket; editable content, lane, ownership,
                        | explicit priority, relationships and archive visibility.
Workflow configuration  | Stable lane/priority references, labels, ordering,
                        | transition rules and designated acceptance transitions.
Configured principal    | Stable identity, actor class and granted authority; its
                        | display name is presentation, never proof of authority.
Comment / acceptance    | Attributed contribution / human decision about a specific
                        | card revision; neither is anonymous mutable presentation.
Policy enforcement      | Evaluate authenticated actor, operation, current domain
                        | state and policy; return allowed result or safe refusal.

Stable identity and mutable presentation
Recommend opaque stable board/card identities and separate human-friendly tickets
unique within a board. Renaming a board, card, lane, priority or user preserves its
identity and references. Tickets are not reused after archive; references include
board identity so identical ticket labels on different boards cannot collide.
Identifier encoding, cross-board moves and legacy reference compatibility remain
later contract decisions. Relationships reference identity rather than titles.

Archive changes default visibility, not existence: preserve identity, content,
comments, relationships and attribution for restoration. Existing references remain
resolvable with an archived indication. Archive-related dependency/triage treatment
belongs to the relationship contract; hiding a card must not silently erase links.
Priority ordering comes from configuration, with derived urgency kept conceptually
distinct from the user's explicit priority. No scoring formula is selected here.

Configured product versus Terry's template
Product mechanism       | Terry-specific configuration/example
Named ordered lanes     | Ready for Work, In Progress, Ready for Review,
                        | Needs Terry, Blocked and owner-controlled Completed.
Transition predicates   | Require In Progress before substantive ticket work;
                        | human-only acceptance and Terry's Completed authority.
Semantic workflow rules | Ordinary dependencies can remain Ready for Work;
                        | Blocked means an external obstacle; Needs Terry means
                        | the next meaningful step requires Terry's judgment.
Priority defaults       | P3 Normal; dependencies alone do not raise priority.

These names and meanings belong in a supplied template, not hardcoded product
identifiers. Explicit owner decisions can release a dependency gate without a
Completed transition, as #0004 demonstrates. A configurable transition alone cannot
prove that off-board work obeyed the In Progress rule; tools must honor that gate.

Actor authority and refusal ownership
Resolve actor identity through the trusted authentication boundary; a client-sent
name, owner field or human/bot flag cannot establish authority. Keep configured
principal grants and policy separate from mutable labels, window preferences and
card prose. Assignment identifies responsibility; it grants no implicit permission.
Administrative policy changes require their own authorized capability and revision
checks. Credential mechanisms and deployment trust boundaries remain later design.

The Go application boundary owns enforcement for every mutation entry point,
including CLI/background actions; UI affordances are advisory. Refuse unknown
actors, missing grants, invalid transitions and stale decisions before domain
mutation. Return a stable sanitized reason and conflict/reconciliation information
where appropriate. Refusal creates no successful domain-change/audit event;
privacy-safe operational diagnostics may record the refusal separately. Policy
administrators own correcting grants; owners resolve workflow choices; clients
preserve user input and present the reason rather than bypassing enforcement.

Atomic human acceptance and invariants
- An authorized human accepts a specific reviewed card revision, not merely its
  current title or ticket. Automation cannot acquire human authority by relabeling.
- Validate actor, acceptance capability, card revision, relevant policy revision
  and transition preconditions together. Commit acceptance evidence, transition
  and attributable change record together, or commit none. Concurrent content or
  policy changes cause a conflict requiring renewed review; no blind replay.
- Subsequent substantive edits cannot inherit that acceptance silently. Retain the
  accepted revision as history and expose the current revision as unaccepted;
  a template may additionally return it to a review lane.
- Identity references remain valid across renames/archive. Missing configuration
  references are validation failures, never substituted by display-name matching.

Assumptions, tradeoffs and open choices
Assume local human/automation collaboration; remote identity guarantees are not
established. Central policy enforcement reduces divergent clients but requires
explicit policy-version conflict handling. Prefer configurable semantic roles over
fixed lane IDs; unrestricted scripting offers flexibility but adds policy risk.
Recommend declarative rules initially. Terry may accept these recommendations as
one packet. Still open: who may administer policy, delegated human acceptance
beyond Terry's template, which edits invalidate review, and whether acceptance
requires reviewer/author separation for humans. Authentication binding, exact
permission vocabulary, persistence and audit reconstruction remain designated
downstream contracts; none prevents this conceptual design.
